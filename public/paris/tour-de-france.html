What’s happening

The final stage covers about 133km from Thoiry into Paris. Once in the city, the peloton runs a circuit of Champs-Élysées laps combined with three passes over the cobbled Côte de la Butte Montmartre, right past Sacré-Cœur, before the finishing sprint back on the Champs-Élysées.

Rollout is roughly 4:30pm. The peloton reaches Paris after about 50km of transfer riding, so expect the first laps through the city from mid-to-late afternoon. The actual finish is closer to 7:30–8pm.

Where to watch

Since you'll already be at Musée d'Orsay that morning, the easiest move is the Quai des Tuileries. It sits right along the route from Concorde toward the Champs-Élysées, so you can catch the riders passing more than once, and it's typically far less packed than the barriers directly on the Champs-Élysées.

Catch one of the earlier laps, around 5–6pm, rather than holding out for the final sprint. That keeps enough time to get back to the 15th for the Le Café du Commerce dinner reservation, which matters more on FIL's last full evening than seeing the very last meter of the race.

If FIL would rather see the actual finish, that's a real option too. It just means pushing dinner to around 9pm and accepting a longer walk back with the closures below.

Getting around that day

Closed all day: George V, Tuileries, Concorde, Champs-Élysées–Clemenceau, Franklin D. Roosevelt, Charles de Gaulle–Étoile, Madeleine.

Parking is banned in the affected zones. Walk or use the RER from outside the closure area. Check transit status the morning of, since barriers and closures typically start early.
