After the breakout success of quizzes, BuzzFeed spun up an experimental Games team to invent the next addictive format, right at the peak of social. The goal was simple: maximize reach. Every game had to be:

1. **Built to share.** Every game ends in a result worth posting.
2. **Flexible for any content.** One format reskins for celebrity, food, style, and more, so each version finds a new audience.
3. **Zero learning curve.** Easy to start, quick to finish.
4. **Mobile-first.** That's where our audience played.

I led design from concept through UI/UX, prototyping, and play-testing to launch, partnering with editors on content design. The team was a producer, an illustrator, three full-stack engineers, and me as product designer. Every game was mobile web, built in JS, and ran on a simple CMS.

---

### Impact

- **60 games, 20+ formats** shipped in 18 months
- **6 games sold** as sponsored content to brands like Google and L'Oréal
- **Hit formats became a game-making tool**, so editors and readers could spin up new games and test new ideas
- **Pop culture content consistently won:** Emoji movie trivia (930k views) and Disney princess mix-and-match (570k views) outperformed handcrafted games like Emoji Oracle (50k views)

---

### A spectrum of formats

The games fell on a spectrum. On one end were **handcrafted games**: one-off builds with custom art and mechanics made around a single idea. On the other were **reskinnable formats**: a proven mechanic that editors could fill with new content using an editor. Most of what scaled lived near that end.


**1. Memory Game** · *Handcrafted* — In "How Long Would You Last as a Barista?" a customer reads off a six-part coffee order. The player taps OK once they've memorized it, then rebuilds the order by tapping ingredients in the right sequence. (390k views)

**2. Mix-and-match** · *In between* — The character is split into top and bottom halves. The player swipes each half independently to combine outfits (arrow keys on desktop), or hits shuffle to randomize, then pins the look. In "Can You Pick A Hipster Outfit For Snow White," she's a New Yorker living in Brooklyn. (570k views)

**5. Text Generator** · *Reskinnable* — The player enters their name (and sometimes picks a favorite character) and gets a character-inspired name to share. Swapping the word bank and art made a new generator for any TV show: "Game of Thrones Name Generator" (250k views), "Orange Is the New Black Tattoo Generator" (180K views).

**6. Roulette** · *Reskinnable* — The player hits Start, photos shuffle, and the spin lands on one: a hairstyle to try in "Hairstyle Roulette," a dinner to cook in "Vegetarian Recipe Roulette." Playful to use and cheap to make, it turned browsing into a game and drove discovery of our lifestyle content.

### Learning

- **Familiar content beats clever mechanics.** Pop culture trivia (930K) outperformed a handcrafted 20-questions game (50K) almost 20 to 1. We doubled down on the formats that worked and dropped the ones that didn't.
- **Design the share, not just the game.** Custom result images and messaging turned every play into a post.
- **Put the tools in editors' hands.** A game-making tool turned editors from requesters into creators, so new games no longer waited on engineering.
- **Autonomy has a cost.** Building our own deploys, CMS, and QA let us move fast but slowed us later. Next time I'd plug into shared infrastructure sooner.
